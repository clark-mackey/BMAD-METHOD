# Gary Bencivenga | Famous "Olive Oil" Sales Letter Breakdown (11/100)

A detailed analysis of Gary Bencivenga's famous olive oil sales letter for the Fresh Pressed Olive Oil Club, breaking down the copywriting techniques that have made it successful for decades.

**Published:** Jan 21, 2021 by Game of Conversions

---

## Key Insights

- Gary Bencivenga's copywriting style is described as 'buttery smooth,' not overly emotional but highly effective because it says 'the right thing at the right time'
- The sales letter uses exclusivity as a key persuasion element, positioning the product as something only available to 'a tiny handful of Americans'
- Bencivenga follows a persuasion equation: urgent problem + unique promise + unquestionable proof + user-friendly proposition = persuasion
- The sales letter effectively combines emotional desire with logical reasoning to build trust and overcome objections
- The offer is structured around the concept of 'affordable luxury,' allowing customers to experience something exclusive without breaking the bank
- Scarcity is introduced early in the copy and reinforced throughout to increase perceived value and create urgency
- The letter uses storytelling to build tension and get readers emotionally invested in the journey of discovering these exclusive olive oils

### Takeaways

- Effective copy doesn't need to be emotionally charged to convert well
- The design of a sales page should complement the product being sold
- Longevity of an ad campaign (running for decades) is strong evidence of its effectiveness
- A clean, spacious layout helps readers focus on the sales message

## Headline Analysis and Opening Elements

> "You are cordially invited to join the tiny handful of Americans who can enjoy rare fresh pressed olive oil all year long."

Csaba analyzes the headline of the sales letter, which positions the offer as an "exclusive invitation" to join a select group of Americans who can enjoy rare fresh pressed olive oil year-round. He breaks down how this headline immediately establishes exclusivity, addresses the unique selling proposition, and handles a potential objection (availability throughout the year) all at once.

The opening elements of the sales letter include a friendly image of TJ Robinson (the face of the company), instant credibility builders, a big promise sub-headline calling these "the best tasting olive oils in the world," and immediate scarcity to increase perceived value. Csaba notes how these elements work together to create both desire and urgency right from the start.

### Takeaways

- Effective headlines can simultaneously create exclusivity, present the USP, and handle objections
- Including scarcity early in copy increases the perceived value of the offer
- Presenting a friendly, relatable face helps build trust with readers
- Preemptively addressing objections (like "Why can't I just buy this at a gourmet store?") strengthens your argument

## The Bencivenga Persuasion Equation

> "Bencivenga persuasion equation which stands for urgent problem plus unique promise plus unquestionable proof plus user-friendly proposition equals persuasion."

Csaba introduces Gary Bencivenga's persuasion equation: **urgent problem + unique promise + unquestionable proof + user-friendly proposition = persuasion**. The sales letter begins by establishing the urgent problem - that it's nearly impossible to get truly high-quality fresh pressed olive oil in America because it spoils quickly, unlike wine which improves with age.

The copy then builds credibility by explaining how Mediterranean cultures celebrate olive oil harvests, which serves a dual purpose of educating readers while also creating an aspirational image of the Mediterranean lifestyle that many readers would want to experience. This section effectively connects the product to a desirable lifestyle, making it about more than just olive oil.

### Takeaways

- A structured persuasion formula helps ensure all necessary elements are included in your copy
- Establishing an urgent problem creates motivation for readers to seek a solution
- Connecting your product to an aspirational lifestyle increases its emotional appeal
- Educating readers about why the problem exists builds credibility and trust

## Copy Structure Analysis and Improvement Opportunities

> "Whenever you are handcuffing copy or you're trying to analyze proven sales letters, you should always think about one thing that could be improved as well."

While analyzing the structure of the sales letter, Csaba points out an improvement opportunity he noticed: the paragraphs are quite long and dense, which can make the copy difficult to read and skim. He suggests that while this style might work for Bencivenga's older, more sophisticated audience, modern copywriters should consider breaking text into shorter paragraphs and using narrower columns for better readability, especially for younger audiences with shorter attention spans.

Csaba compares this to the structure of successful email marketing, noting that the best email marketers today use narrower structures with larger fonts (16-18pt) to improve readability, particularly on mobile devices. This section provides practical advice for adapting proven techniques to contemporary audiences and platforms.

### Takeaways

- Even successful copy can have areas for improvement
- Paragraph length and line width significantly impact readability
- Copy structure should be adapted based on the target audience's reading preferences
- Modern digital formats often require more white space and shorter paragraphs than traditional print copy
- Always consider how your copy will appear on different devices and screens

## Philosophical Questions and Story Elements

> "You and I insist on fresh milk and fresh fish, why not fresh olive oil?"

Csaba highlights the effective use of a philosophical question in the sales letter: "You and I insist on fresh milk and fresh fish, why not fresh olive oil?" This question serves as a powerful transition into the story of TJ Robinson's journey to discover and source the best olive oils. The question makes readers think "that sounds reasonable" and opens their minds to the product's value proposition.

Following this question, the sales letter delves into TJ Robinson's **discovery story**, positioning him as an advocate for the reader who went on a quest to solve the problem of accessing fresh olive oil. The story creates tension and emotional investment as readers root for TJ to succeed in his mission. Csaba notes how the testimonials are strategically placed to reinforce the main narrative rather than appearing random.

### Takeaways

- Philosophical questions can create natural agreement and open readers' minds
- Positioning your product creator as an advocate for the customer builds rapport
- Creating tension in your story gets readers emotionally invested in the outcome
- Strategic placement of testimonials should reinforce your main narrative points

## Call to Action and Offer Structure

> "RSVP today. I like this because again it creates a sense of exclusivity to it."

The sales letter's call to action uses "RSVP today" rather than a typical "Buy Now" button, which Csaba notes reinforces the exclusive, invitation-only feeling of the offer. The structure presents two options (different bottle sizes) rather than a yes/no decision, making it easier for prospects to commit by focusing on which option to choose rather than whether to buy at all.

The offer is strengthened with clear bonuses that enhance the experience, including reports on how to enjoy the oils and suggestions for food pairings. Csaba highlights a particularly effective benefit: instructions for hosting an olive oil tasting party, which appeals to the deeper desire of many customers to be perceived as sophisticated hosts with excellent taste. This transforms the product from simply being about olive oil to being about status and identity.

### Takeaways

- Using language like "RSVP" instead of "Buy Now" can reinforce exclusivity
- Offering choice between options (rather than yes/no) increases conversion
- The best bonuses enhance the core experience of the product
- Appealing to customers' desire for status and identity is more powerful than focusing on product features
- Clear money-back guarantees help overcome remaining hesitations

---

## The Affordable Luxury Angle

> "This is one of those little luxuries at a most reasonable price that can add such delight and satisfaction and comfort to every meal you serve and partake. We all deserve such simple daily pleasures these days."

Csaba analyzes the closing argument of the sales letter, which positions the olive oil subscription as an "affordable luxury" - a concept that has proven effective for brands like Starbucks. The copy acknowledges that while $50 for a bottle of olive oil might seem expensive compared to supermarket options, it represents an accessible way to experience luxury and the Mediterranean lifestyle.

The final line, "We all deserve such simple daily pleasures these days," resonates deeply with busy people who have many responsibilities and feel they deserve small indulgences. This framing helps overcome price objections by connecting the purchase to self-care and deserved rewards rather than mere consumption. Csaba notes how powerful this closing sentiment is for motivating the final decision to purchase.

### Takeaways

- Positioning products as "affordable luxury" helps justify premium pricing
- Connecting purchases to deserved rewards overcomes price resistance
- The closing sentiment of copy should address the emotional "permission" to buy
- Acknowledging customers' hard work and responsibilities creates empathy and connection

## Conclusion

Gary Bencivenga's olive oil sales letter demonstrates why he's considered one of the greatest copywriters of all time. His systematic approach to persuasion combines emotional appeals with logical reasoning, creating copy that feels natural rather than pushy while still driving impressive conversions.

The letter's brilliance lies in how it transforms a simple product (olive oil) into an experience that connects with deeper desires - to live well, to impress others, to treat oneself to affordable luxury, and to participate in an exclusive club. This transformation from selling a product to selling an identity and experience is what separates average copy from breakthrough campaigns that run for decades.

**So what?** As marketers and copywriters, we should look beyond the surface techniques to understand the deeper psychology at work. The most effective persuasion doesn't come from clever tactics but from genuinely understanding what people want beyond the physical product - status, identity, experiences, and small daily pleasures that make life more meaningful. When we connect our offers to these deeper motivations while providing logical justification, we create irresistible propositions that stand the test of time.
